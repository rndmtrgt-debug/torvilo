Important: This is an estimate using figures you enter. It does not determine your legal award rate, classification, allowances, overtime or tax.

How the income target calculator works

Enter the gross income target you want to model, your hourly rate and a rate multiplier. TORVILO divides the target by the effective hourly rate to estimate how many paid hours would be needed to reach that amount.

Planning with different rates

You can change the hourly rate or multiplier to compare scenarios. A multiplier of 1 uses the entered hourly rate as-is, while a higher multiplier models a higher effective rate.

Useful next steps

To estimate one specific shift, use the Shift Pay Calculator. To record hours you have actually worked, use the Shift Tracker. You can also read Understanding Australian pay rates before choosing a rate to enter.

About the result

The result is a planning estimate based only on the target, rate and multiplier you provide. It does not account for unpaid breaks, changing rates across a period, tax, allowances or other payroll adjustments.

Gross target versus money in your bank

The target is gross income before tax. If your real goal is a particular amount of take-home pay, first estimate the gross income required and then check the result with the Take-home Pay Calculator. Tax circumstances can mean a $500 gross target does not produce $500 of spendable income.

Worked example

At an entered rate of $30 per hour and a 1× multiplier, a $500 gross target requires about 16.67 paid hours. At an entered effective rate of $45 per hour, the same gross target requires about 11.11 paid hours. These are planning comparisons, not predictions of available shifts.

Use realistic paid hours

The result counts paid hours only. If a workday includes unpaid breaks, travel or waiting time, the elapsed time needed to reach a target can be longer. Gig workers should also consider operating costs rather than comparing platform revenue directly with employee gross wages.

Turn a target into a weekly plan

After calculating the hours needed, compare them with the number of shifts you realistically expect to work. Keep completed shifts in the Shift Tracker so you can compare your plan with what actually happened.

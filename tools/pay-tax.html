Australian Take-Home Pay Calculator

Estimate take-home pay from gross earnings, with income tax, Medicare levy and employer super shown separately.

Enter your gross pay and frequency to calculate.
Estimate only: Uses 2026–27 Australian resident individual income-tax rates, assumes the full tax-free threshold, and uses a simplified Medicare levy estimate. Employer super uses 12%. It applies the Low Income Tax Offset where eligible and can estimate a 2026–27 study/training loan repayment. Family Medicare thresholds, Medicare levy surcharge, deductions, other offsets and salary sacrifice are not modelled. Study-loan repayment income is assumed equal to gross earnings; this may not reflect your circumstances.

How it works

TORVILO annualises the gross pay you enter, estimates resident income tax and the Medicare levy, then converts the estimated take-home amount back to your selected pay frequency. Employer super is displayed separately rather than deducted from take-home pay.

Use the result as an estimate

Actual payroll withholding and final tax can differ because individual circumstances matter. For payroll-level figures, check the Australian Taxation Office's current PAYG withholding information.

Worked 2026–27 example

Suppose you earn $1,500 gross each week throughout the year. Annualised gross earnings are $78,000. Under the resident tax brackets, tax before offsets is $13,920. The low-income tax offset does not apply at this income. A simple 2% Medicare levy estimate adds $1,560, giving an estimated annual take-home amount of $62,520, or about $1,202.31 per week, before any study-loan repayment or other adjustments. The separate indicative 12% employer-super figure is $9,360 annually if all earnings qualify.

Study and training loans: an important distinction

Compulsory study-loan repayments are calculated using repayment income, which can differ from gross salary or taxable income. Reportable super contributions, fringe benefits and certain investment losses can affect repayment income. TORVILO assumes repayment income equals annualised gross earnings, so the optional figure is only a simplified scenario. Your employer's withholding may differ from the final repayment assessed by the ATO.

Official 2026–27 sources

Check the ATO resident tax rates, Australian Government study-loan repayment explanation, and ATO 2026 PAYG withholding tables. The calculation is an annual tax-planning estimate, not a replacement for payroll withholding tables or individual tax advice.

Weekly versus fortnightly take-home pay

Choose the frequency that matches the gross amount you enter. For example, $1,500 weekly and $3,000 fortnightly both represent $78,000 gross annually when paid consistently across 52 weeks or 26 fortnights. The estimated annual result should therefore be the same under identical Medicare and loan settings; only the displayed per-pay amount changes.

Frequently asked questions

Is this my exact payslip amount?

No. This tool estimates an annual income-tax outcome and spreads it across pay periods. Employer PAYG withholding tables, other income, deductions and individual circumstances can change actual payslips.

Does employer super reduce my take-home pay?

Employer super is shown separately and is not subtracted from the estimated cash take-home amount. Salary sacrifice or packages that include super need separate consideration.

What this estimate includes — and what it does not

The calculator starts by converting the selected pay frequency to an annual gross-income estimate. It then applies the resident income-tax calculation used by TORVILO, the Low Income Tax Offset where eligible, and optional simplified Medicare and study/training-loan estimates. The annual result is converted back to the frequency you selected.

This is useful for comparing scenarios, but it is deliberately not a tax-return calculator. It does not ask about deductions, investment income, salary packaging, family circumstances or every levy and offset that can affect a final assessment.

Why your payslip can be different

Employers generally withhold PAYG amounts during the year rather than calculating your final annual tax liability on every payday. Payroll tables, rounding and information you have given your employer can therefore produce a different pay-period figure from a planning estimate.

Super is not take-home pay

The employer-super figure is displayed separately. It is not added to the cash amount you receive and is not subtracted as income tax. The actual super obligation can also depend on which earnings are ordinary time earnings.

Before making a money decision

Use this result as a starting point for a budget or pay comparison, then check your actual payslip and current ATO information. For help understanding the lines on a payslip, read How to read your payslip. For employer super, see Superannuation basics.

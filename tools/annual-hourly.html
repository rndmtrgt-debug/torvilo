How the conversion works

The annual salary is divided by the paid weeks you enter and then by your paid hours per week. This gives an equivalent gross hourly rate for comparing different working patterns.

Annual salary to hourly rate: examples

Assuming 38 paid hours a week for 52 paid weeks, a $60,000 annual base salary is approximately $30.36 per hour; $70,000 is $35.43; $80,000 is $40.49; and $90,000 is $45.55. These are equivalent gross rates, not necessarily the legal hourly rate in a contract or award.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the hourly figure change with weekly hours?

Because the same annual salary is divided across more or fewer paid hours. When comparing two roles, use the hours you realistically expect to work, and separately check whether overtime applies.

Should I subtract paid annual leave?

Not if your salary continues during that leave and the annual amount already includes it. Use the paid-weeks field consistently; do not subtract paid leave twice.

What if the advertised package includes super?

Ask for the base cash salary excluding super and other package components. For a wider comparison, read how to compare job offers or use our free worksheet.

Why an equivalent hourly rate is useful

An annual salary can be difficult to compare with an hourly job because the units are different. Converting salary to an equivalent hourly figure creates a common starting point, particularly when comparing roles with different expected weekly hours.

Compare the same salary at different hours

At $70,000 annual base salary over 52 paid weeks, 38 paid hours per week gives approximately $35.43 an hour. If the role actually requires 45 hours every week, the effective gross rate falls to approximately $29.91 an hour. The calculator cannot decide whether additional hours are reasonable or attract overtime; check your contract and the relevant award or agreement.

Salary packaging caution: when a job advertisement quotes a total remuneration package including 12% employer super, the cash base salary is not the same as the package amount. Confirm the components before entering the annual salary.

Worked example

A $70,000 annual salary spread across 52 paid weeks and 38 paid hours per week represents 1,976 paid hours. Dividing $70,000 by 1,976 gives an equivalent gross rate of about $35.43 per hour. This is a mathematical comparison, not an award-rate determination.

Salary package versus base salary

Before entering a figure, check whether the advertised amount is base salary or a package that includes super or other benefits. Entering a total package as though it were cash salary can overstate the equivalent hourly wage.

Hours can change the comparison

Two jobs with the same annual salary can have different effective hourly values if their expected working hours differ. Also consider paid leave, overtime arrangements and other conditions rather than choosing between jobs from this number alone.

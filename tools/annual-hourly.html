How the conversion works

The annual salary is divided by the paid weeks you enter and then by your paid hours per week. This gives an equivalent gross hourly rate for comparing different working patterns.

Why an equivalent hourly rate is useful

An annual salary can be difficult to compare with an hourly job because the units are different. Converting salary to an equivalent hourly figure creates a common starting point, particularly when comparing roles with different expected weekly hours.

Worked example

A $70,000 annual salary spread across 52 paid weeks and 38 paid hours per week represents 1,976 paid hours. Dividing $70,000 by 1,976 gives an equivalent gross rate of about $35.43 per hour. This is a mathematical comparison, not an award-rate determination.

Salary package versus base salary

Before entering a figure, check whether the advertised amount is base salary or a package that includes super or other benefits. Entering a total package as though it were cash salary can overstate the equivalent hourly wage.

Hours can change the comparison

Two jobs with the same annual salary can have different effective hourly values if their expected working hours differ. Also consider paid leave, overtime arrangements and other conditions rather than choosing between jobs from this number alone.

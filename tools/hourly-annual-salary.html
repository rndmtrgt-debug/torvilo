How the conversion works

TORVILO multiplies your hourly rate by your paid hours per week to estimate weekly gross income. Annual income is weekly income multiplied by the number of paid weeks you enter. Fortnightly income is two weeks, and the monthly figure is the annual estimate divided by 12.

Why paid weeks are adjustable

Not everyone is paid for 52 weeks each year. Casual, seasonal and contract workers may have unpaid periods. Changing the paid-weeks field makes the annual estimate more useful for different working patterns.

Compare different paid-work patterns

For a $30 hourly rate and 38 paid hours a week, working 52 paid weeks produces $59,280 gross annually. Working 46 paid weeks instead produces $52,440. This $6,840 difference matters for casual and seasonal workers who may not be paid during time off.

Check what “paid weeks” means: paid annual leave is ordinarily included in a permanent employee's annual salary, while unpaid leave and irregular casual work may reduce actual earnings. Don't deduct annual-leave weeks twice.

Example

At $30 per hour for 38 paid hours each week over 52 paid weeks, the estimate is $1,140 per week and $59,280 per year before tax.

Hourly pay to annual salary: quick comparison

At 38 paid hours per week across 52 paid weeks, a $25 hourly rate equals $49,400 gross annually; $30 equals $59,280; $35 equals $69,160; and $40 equals $79,040. These are gross amounts before tax and assume the same hourly rate for every paid hour. Overtime and penalty rates can change the outcome.

Common questions

Does the calculation include super?

The annual gross salary figure is cash earnings before tax. The separate 12% employer super estimate is illustrative and is not added to the gross cash salary. Super obligations depend on eligible earnings.

What if I work casual hours?

Use a realistic estimate of average paid hours and weeks rather than automatically choosing 52. For a fuller comparison, see casual versus permanent pay.

Can I compare two jobs?

Yes, but compare expected hours, paid leave, super arrangements and commuting costs as well as annual gross income. Follow our job offer comparison guide and downloadable worksheet.

Gross salary is not take-home salary

The weekly, fortnightly, monthly and annual conversions begin with gross earnings. They do not mean the same amount will reach your bank account. Income tax and other circumstances can change net pay, which is why TORVILO shows tax estimates separately.

What happens if your hours change?

An hourly worker's annual earnings can move substantially when weekly hours vary. If you normally work 38 hours but some weeks are shorter, entering 38 hours for all 52 weeks can overstate annual income. Use a realistic average or reduce paid weeks when modelling unpaid periods.

Comparing an hourly job with a salary

Converting both options to annual gross income can be useful, but it is only one part of a job comparison. Consider expected hours, paid leave, employment type, overtime arrangements and super as well. A higher headline annual figure does not automatically mean a better effective hourly outcome.

How to use this calculator

Enter the base hourly rate you have verified, then split your paid hours across ordinary time, 1.5×, 2× and an optional custom multiplier. TORVILO calculates each category separately and adds them together.

Why the multiplier is not chosen automatically

Australian penalty and overtime rates are not one universal set of percentages. The rate can depend on the award, registered agreement, employment type, when the work occurs and other circumstances. Fair Work says penalty rates may apply to weekends, public holidays, overtime, late nights and early mornings.

Check the Fair Work Ombudsman penalty rates information and the award or agreement that applies to you.

Example

If your verified base rate is $30 per hour and you enter 8 ordinary hours plus 2 hours at 1.5×, the estimate is $240 ordinary pay plus $90 overtime pay, for $330 gross before tax.

Split mixed-rate shifts instead of averaging

If different portions of work attract different verified rates, enter those hours separately. This makes the calculation easier to audit later because you can see the ordinary component and each higher-rate component rather than relying on one blended hourly figure.

Example with three rate categories

Using a $30 base rate purely as an arithmetic example, 6 ordinary hours would produce $180, 2 hours entered at 1.5× would produce $90, and 1 hour entered at 2× would produce $60. The combined gross estimate would be $330. The example does not establish that those multipliers apply to a particular worker.

Common reasons a real payslip may differ

The applicable award or agreement may define overtime boundaries differently, use different penalty percentages, include casual loading in a particular way, or apply allowances and minimum-engagement rules. A shift crossing different time bands can also need to be divided differently.

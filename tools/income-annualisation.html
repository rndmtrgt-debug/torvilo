Income Annualisation Calculator

Convert gross income earned over a known period into an annualised income estimate, even when your pay varies.

Enter the number of periods covered by the earnings, not the number of payslips if your pay frequency changed. Include unpaid periods when estimating a continuing average.

Enter gross income and the period it covers.
Planning estimate only. Assumes the average continues for a full year. This is not a prediction of future income, a lender assessment, or an ATO tax or payroll annualisation calculation. Super, deductions and tax are excluded.

How to annualise income

Divide the total gross earnings by the number of periods they cover, then multiply by the number of equivalent periods in a year. TORVILO uses 52 weeks, 26 fortnights or 12 months as standard planning conventions.

Annualised income = gross earnings ÷ elapsed periods × periods per year.

Using year-to-date earnings from a payslip

Identify the start and end dates covered by the gross year-to-date total. Enter the gross amount and the elapsed weeks or months. For instance, $18,000 earned across 15 weeks gives an average of $1,200 per week and an annualised estimate of $62,400. Use the actual coverage of the figure; do not assume that your employer's year-to-date total includes earnings from previous employers.

Irregular income and unpaid time

If $12,000 was earned over 12 elapsed weeks, including two unpaid weeks, the observed average is $1,000 per week, or $52,000 annualised. Dividing by only the ten paid weeks instead would give $62,400, overstating the observed calendar-week average. For seasonal work, compare a longer representative period and use a separate budget scenario for expected unpaid time.

Annualised income versus annual salary

An annual salary is a contractual amount for a specified period; an annualised income estimate extrapolates observed earnings. Bonuses, overtime, changing hours, seasonal work or a recent pay rise can make the two differ. If you know your hourly rate and expected paid weeks, use the hourly to annual salary calculator. If you know a base salary, use the annual salary to hourly calculator.

What about take-home pay?

These results are gross income before deductions. For a separate estimate of income tax and Medicare, enter an appropriate gross pay figure in the take-home pay calculator. Do not use an extrapolated income as proof of actual future earnings. See our irregular income budgeting guide and free planning worksheets.

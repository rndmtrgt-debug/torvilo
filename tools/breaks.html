Important: This is an estimate using figures you enter. It does not determine your legal award rate, classification, allowances, overtime or tax.

How to calculate paid hours

Enter the total length of your shift in hours and the length of your unpaid break in minutes. TORVILO converts the break to hours and subtracts it from the shift length to show the remaining paid hours.

Why paid hours matter

Keeping a clear record of paid hours can make it easier to compare your own shift records with a payslip or pay estimate. Whether a particular break is paid or unpaid depends on the rules that apply to your employment.

Useful next steps

Once you know your paid hours, use the Shift Pay Calculator to estimate gross earnings or save completed work in the Shift Tracker. If you are checking an actual payment, see How to check your pay.

About this estimate

This calculator only subtracts the break duration you enter. It does not decide whether a break should legally be paid, when a break must be taken, or which award or agreement applies.

Worked example

A 9-hour shift with a 30-minute unpaid break contains 8.5 paid hours: 30 minutes is 0.5 hours, so 9 − 0.5 = 8.5. If there are multiple unpaid breaks, add their minutes together before entering the total.

Paid and unpaid breaks are not interchangeable

This tool assumes the break duration you enter is unpaid because that is the arithmetic it has been asked to perform. It does not determine whether an employer is entitled to treat a particular break as unpaid. Break entitlements can depend on the award, agreement and working arrangement.

Why small differences matter

A 15-minute difference is 0.25 hours. Across repeated shifts, small time differences can accumulate. Keeping start, finish and break records makes it easier to explain why your paid-hours total differs from a roster or payslip.

Use the result in a pay check

Once you know paid hours, compare them with the hours listed for the same pay period. Our payslip guide explains how to compare hours, rates, gross pay and deductions without mixing them together.

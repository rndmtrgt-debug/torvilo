How the shift pay calculator works

Enter your hourly rate, total shift length, unpaid break and rate multiplier. TORVILO subtracts the unpaid break from the shift length, then multiplies the paid hours by your hourly rate and multiplier to estimate gross shift pay before tax.

What is a rate multiplier?

A multiplier lets you model a rate that is higher than your base hourly rate. For example, a multiplier of 1.5 calculates one-and-a-half times the hourly rate. The correct rate or multiplier depends on your employment circumstances, so verify it before relying on the estimate.

Tax and super

The result includes a simplified 2026–27 resident income-tax estimate and a 12% employer super estimate. Actual PAYG withholding and final tax can differ. Super is shown separately from take-home pay and may not apply to every component of earnings.

Worked shift example

Suppose a shift lasts 8 hours with a 30-minute unpaid break. That leaves 7.5 paid hours. At an entered rate of $30 per hour and a 1× multiplier, the gross-pay arithmetic is 7.5 × $30 = $225 before tax. If a verified higher multiplier applies, changing the multiplier lets you model that scenario.

Three things to check before relying on a result

First, make sure the shift length and unpaid break match what you actually worked. Second, verify the hourly rate for the relevant date. Third, verify any multiplier against the award, agreement or other arrangement covering the work. The calculator cannot infer those legal details from a job title alone.

When one multiplier is not enough

A shift can contain different rate periods. For example, some hours might be ordinary and others might fall into an overtime or penalty category. Instead of forcing the whole shift through one multiplier, use the Overtime & Penalty Rate Calculator to split the hours.

Checking the result against a payslip

Match the same pay period and compare paid hours and gross earnings before focusing on net pay. Tax deductions can make the bank deposit look different even when the gross-pay arithmetic is correct. Our payslip guide walks through that comparison.

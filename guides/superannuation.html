Superannuation: what your pay calculator is showing

Super is money for retirement and is usually an employer contribution on eligible earnings. It is separate from the income-tax and take-home-pay figures workers often focus on each payday.

The current Super Guarantee rate

For eligible employees, the Super Guarantee rate is 12% from 1 July 2025. The actual contribution is based on the earnings to which the Super Guarantee rules apply, commonly described through ordinary time earnings (OTE).

Why TORVILO shows super separately

TORVILO's Take-home Pay Calculator shows an employer-super estimate separately from estimated take-home pay. It does not subtract employer super from your estimated net pay. This helps avoid treating employer super as though it were ordinary PAYG withholding.

Not every payment is treated identically

Whether a payment forms part of OTE can depend on what the payment is for. This is one reason a simple percentage of every dollar appearing on a payslip may not reproduce an employer's exact super obligation.

Check more than the payslip

A payslip can show super information, but it is also useful to check your super fund account and compare contributions over time. Timing rules mean a contribution does not necessarily appear in your fund account on the same day as your wages.

If you change jobs

Keep track of which super fund your employer is paying into and make sure your employer has the details needed to direct contributions correctly. Consolidation and fund choice can have financial consequences, so use official information before making decisions.

Where TORVILO fits

Use TORVILO for a planning estimate, then use your payslip, super fund records and current ATO information to verify actual contributions. If you are checking a pay period as a whole, our payslip guide provides a practical checklist.

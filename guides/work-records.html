WORKER GUIDE

Keeping useful work and pay records

A good personal record does not need to duplicate a payroll system. It needs enough detail to let you reconstruct what happened when a roster, payslip or bank deposit does not match your memory.

What to record after a shift

Record the date, actual start and finish times, unpaid breaks and the paid hours you expect. If the shift includes a weekend, public holiday, overtime period or another feature that may affect the rate, make a short note while the details are fresh.

If you know the rate being applied, recording it can make later comparisons faster. TORVILO's Shift Tracker stores a simple date, paid-hours, hourly-rate and tips record locally in your browser and can export the entries to CSV.

Keep the source documents too

Your own notes are most useful when they can be compared with source documents. Keep relevant rosters, payslips and employment documents. Fair Work says employers must keep certain employee records and time-and-wages records for 7 years, but maintaining your own organised records gives you an independent comparison.

Match records by pay period

Do not compare an arbitrary calendar week with a fortnightly payslip. Start with the pay-period dates printed on the payslip, then gather only the shifts that fall into that period. This avoids creating an apparent discrepancy simply because a shift belongs to the next payroll cycle.

Separate hours, rates and deductions

Check the calculation in stages. First compare hours. Then compare the rate or rate categories. Then compare gross earnings. Finally look at deductions and net pay. Working in that order makes it easier to identify whether a difference comes from time records, wage arithmetic or deductions.

Make backups part of the routine

Local browser records can disappear if browser data is cleared or a device is replaced. Export important records periodically and store them somewhere you control. A CSV is useful because it can be opened by common spreadsheet applications without depending on TORVILO.

When you find a difference

Write down the exact date, hours, rate and payslip line involved. A specific discrepancy is easier to investigate than saying only that a total “looks wrong”. Recheck the applicable award or agreement and current rate before raising the issue with payroll or the employer.

What employers must record

Fair Work states that employers have record-keeping and payslip obligations, including records about pay and, in relevant circumstances, hours worked. Official requirements are more detailed than this personal-record guide, so use Fair Work for the authoritative rules.

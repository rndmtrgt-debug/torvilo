How to calculate annual leave accrual in Australia

Convert your annual entitlement into hours each pay period, then compare the estimate with your payslip and leave records.

Start with ordinary hours, not total hours worked

Under the National Employment Standards, full-time and part-time employees generally accrue four weeks of paid annual leave a year based on ordinary hours. Certain shiftworkers receive five weeks. Casual employees do not receive paid annual leave under the NES. Confirm your entitlement and any more generous agreement before calculating.

Annual leave accrual formula

For a regular roster, annual entitlement in hours = ordinary weekly hours × annual entitlement in weeks. Divide that figure by 52 for an approximate weekly planning rate, or multiply the weekly amount by two for a fortnight. Actual accrual is progressive and payroll systems may calculate using days or hours.

Keep unrounded figures when adding many periods; rounding each fortnight can create a small difference.

Check a balance across two payslips

Suppose your opening balance is 44 hours, you accrue approximately 5.846 hours over a fortnight and take 7.6 hours of annual leave. The estimated closing balance is 42.246 hours, displayed as 42.25. Compare that with the actual closing balance. An additional adjustment, a different accrual basis or leave taken in another period can explain a difference.

What can change the estimate?

  • Changing ordinary hours: calculate each roster period separately.
  • Unpaid absences: some do not count towards accrual; exceptions apply.
  • Shiftworker entitlements or more generous agreements: use the verified number of weeks rather than assuming four.
  • Leave loading affects payment, not the number of leave hours accrued.

Before querying payroll

Gather the opening and closing balances, the exact pay-period dates, ordinary hours and leave taken. Ask which accrual basis and adjustments were applied. Check leave payment separately from leave hours: annual leave is ordinarily paid at base pay for ordinary hours, and an award or agreement can provide loading or other payments.

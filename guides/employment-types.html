WORKER GUIDE

Casual, part-time and full-time: what changes?

Employment type affects more than the label on a contract. It can change how pay, leave, hours and other entitlements work, so it is an important starting point when checking a wage.

Full-time employment

Full-time employees generally work an average of 38 hours a week, although the applicable award or agreement and reasonable additional hours can matter. They usually receive paid leave entitlements such as annual leave and personal/carer's leave.

Part-time employment

Part-time employees work fewer than 38 hours a week and generally receive the same types of minimum entitlements as full-time employees on a proportional basis. Awards can also contain rules about agreed patterns of work and changes to hours.

Casual employment

Casual employment operates differently. Casual employees generally receive a casual loading or a specific casual rate instead of some paid leave entitlements. Whether a worker is casual is not determined simply by whether their roster changes from week to week; Australian workplace law contains a specific definition and rules.

Why this matters when comparing hourly rates

A casual hourly rate can look higher than a permanent employee's base hourly rate because the casual rate can include loading. Comparing only the headline hourly number can therefore be misleading. Compare employment type, classification and the applicable award or agreement before deciding one rate is better or worse.

Penalty rates can also differ

Awards can specify different weekend, public-holiday or other penalty outcomes for casual and permanent employees. Do not assume that adding a standard percentage to any base rate reproduces the legal result for every award.

What to check in your own job

Identify your employment type, award or agreement and classification. Then use the current official pay guide or Pay and Conditions Tool for the period you are checking. If your contract label and the way the job actually operates appear inconsistent, get current Fair Work information rather than relying on a calculator.

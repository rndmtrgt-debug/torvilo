WORKER GUIDE

How to read your payslip

A payslip is more useful when you can connect every figure on it to the hours you worked, the rate that applied and the money that reached your account.

Start with the pay period

Check the start and end dates first. When comparing your own records with payroll, only include shifts that fall inside the same pay period. A shift near the cut-off can appear on a later payslip depending on the employer's payroll process.

Gross pay and net pay are different

Gross pay is earnings before deductions. Net pay is what remains after deductions such as PAYG withholding. A difference between gross and the amount deposited into your bank account is therefore not automatically an error.

Match hours and rates

Compare the hours shown with your roster, timesheet or personal records. If the payslip separates ordinary, overtime, weekend or public-holiday hours, check each category separately. A total-hours check can miss a shift that was paid at the wrong rate.

TORVILO's Paid Hours Calculator can help you calculate paid time after an unpaid break, while the Shift Pay Calculator can model a rate and multiplier you have already verified.

Look at deductions individually

Do not treat every deduction as tax. Read the description beside each deduction and compare PAYG withholding separately from any other authorised deduction. TORVILO's Take-home Pay Calculator is an estimate for planning; it is not a replacement for payroll withholding calculations.

Check super information

Payslips can include superannuation contribution information. Super is separate from take-home pay and generally should not be treated as an amount deducted from the net-pay estimate produced by TORVILO. See our superannuation guide for the basics.

A practical five-minute check

For each pay period, compare dates, paid hours, hourly or salary rates, penalty/overtime categories, gross pay, deductions and net pay. Keep the payslip with your own records so you can identify patterns rather than relying on memory weeks later.

If something looks wrong

Recheck the pay period and your records first. Then identify the exact line or shift that differs and raise that specific discrepancy with payroll or your employer. For minimum entitlements and payslip requirements, use current Fair Work Ombudsman information.

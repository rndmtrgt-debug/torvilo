PAY EXPLAINER

Gross pay, net pay and super: what is the difference?

Three figures can describe the same job without meaning the same thing. Keeping gross earnings, take-home pay and employer super separate makes pay comparisons much clearer.

Gross pay

Gross pay is earnings before deductions. When an hourly worker multiplies paid hours by an hourly rate, the starting result is generally a gross-pay figure. Gross pay is therefore the useful figure when checking whether hours and rates have been multiplied correctly.

Net or take-home pay

Net pay is the amount remaining after deductions shown through payroll. PAYG withholding can be one of those deductions. That is why a $1,000 gross-pay figure should not be expected to produce a $1,000 bank deposit.

PAYG withholding is not necessarily final tax

Amounts withheld through payroll are part of the tax system during the year. Your final tax position can depend on your annual circumstances. This is one reason a general calculator can provide a planning estimate without reproducing every employer's exact pay-period withholding.

Employer super is separate again

Employer super contributions are for retirement savings and are not the same as take-home wages. When TORVILO displays an employer-super estimate, it keeps that figure separate rather than subtracting it as though it were income tax.

A simple comparison workflow

If you are checking a payslip, start with paid hours and applicable rates to understand gross pay. Then examine the deductions that move the figure from gross to net. Finally check super information separately. This avoids trying to diagnose several different calculations from the bank deposit alone.

Casual vs Permanent Pay: Compare the Whole Offer

A practical Australian comparison of casual loading, paid leave, hours, super and income certainty.

Start with equivalent hours

A casual rate can look higher because it may include a casual loading. Compare the actual hourly rate, the paid hours you expect to receive and any award or agreement provisions. A casual employee generally does not receive paid annual leave in the same way as a permanent employee; some casual employees can access other entitlements. Avoid assuming a loading replaces every entitlement.

A worked example

Suppose a permanent role pays $30 per hour for 38 hours across 52 paid weeks: the annual base earnings are $59,280. A casual role pays $37.50 per hour for 38 hours across 46 paid weeks: the annual gross is $65,550. The casual role is higher by $6,270 in this example, but hours may not be guaranteed, and the comparison does not put a dollar value on paid leave, sick days or security. If the casual worker averages 30 paid hours over 46 weeks, annual gross falls to $51,750.

Compare like with like

Write down the applicable award or agreement, classification, base rate, casual loading, penalty rates, guaranteed or likely hours, paid leave, roster stability, employer super and unreimbursed costs. Do not add super to one offer while comparing it with the other offer's base wage.

What can change the answer

Weekend and public holiday work, overtime, broken shifts, allowances, leave arrangements and changes to rostered hours can all matter. Tax withholding can differ even if annual gross amounts are similar.

Verify the conditions

Use the Fair Work Ombudsman casual employment guidance and the applicable award or enterprise agreement. The examples above are illustrative, not an award-rate quote.

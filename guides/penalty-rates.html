Penalty rates and overtime: how to check the right number

There is no single weekend or overtime multiplier that applies to every Australian worker. The correct result depends on the workplace instrument and circumstances that cover the work.

Penalty rate and overtime are not interchangeable

A penalty rate can apply because of when work is performed, such as a weekend or public holiday. Overtime generally relates to hours worked outside ordinary-hour rules. The definitions and rates come from the applicable award, agreement or other workplace rules.

Why one universal calculator would be misleading

Awards can use different percentages, time bands and rules. Employment type and classification can also affect the outcome. Some provisions use an hourly percentage while others can include additional amounts or conditions. For that reason, TORVILO separates finding the legal rate from doing the arithmetic.

Step 1: identify what covers you

Find the applicable award or registered agreement and your classification. If you are award and agreement free, different minimum-wage rules can apply. Fair Work's pay guides summarise common minimum rates and penalties for awards.

Step 2: identify the relevant hours

Write down the date, start and finish time and unpaid breaks. Note whether the shift crosses midnight or moves between different time bands. Splitting a shift into sections can be necessary when different rates apply to different hours.

Step 3: verify the rate, then calculate

Once you have verified the applicable rate or multiplier, use the Overtime & Penalty Calculator to estimate the arithmetic. For a single shift, the Shift Pay Calculator can also model paid hours after an unpaid break.

Worked arithmetic example

If a verified hourly rate were $30 and a verified multiplier were 1.5 for 4 hours, the arithmetic component would be $30 × 1.5 × 4 = $180. That example demonstrates multiplication only; it does not say that 1.5 is the legally applicable multiplier for a particular job.

Where to verify

Use the Fair Work Ombudsman's current pay guides and Pay and Conditions Tool. Check the version applying to the date worked because rates and award provisions can change.

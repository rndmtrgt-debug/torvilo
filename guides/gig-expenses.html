WORKER GUIDE

Gig work income and expense records

For delivery and other gig work, recording income, time, kilometres and costs can help you understand what a session actually earned and keep information available for tax time.

Record each work session

A simple session record can include the date, platform income, tips or bonuses, hours online, kilometres driven, fuel and other costs you want to track. Recording the information while it is fresh is usually easier than reconstructing it later.

Gross income is not the same as operating profit

The amount paid by a platform does not show what remains after the costs of doing the work. TORVILO's Delivery Profit Calculator subtracts fuel and other entered session costs from income and also shows estimated profit per hour and per kilometre.

Keep evidence as well as totals

A personal spreadsheet or calculator total is useful for organisation, but supporting records can also matter. Keep relevant platform statements, invoices, receipts and other evidence needed to support the income and expenses you report.

Vehicle records

If you use a car for gig work, keeping a consistent record of work-related travel and vehicle costs can make later calculations easier. The tax method and evidence requirements that apply depend on your circumstances, so use current Australian Taxation Office guidance rather than treating a profitability calculation as a tax deduction calculation.

Separate business tracking from tax treatment

You may choose to include a cost when measuring whether a delivery session was worthwhile, but that does not automatically make the same amount tax deductible. Tax deductions have their own eligibility and record-keeping rules.

Keep records organised

Consider organising records by financial year and keeping clear links between a transaction and its supporting document. Platform summaries can help with reconciliation, but your own records can provide additional detail about individual sessions.

Official tax information

See the Australian Taxation Office record-keeping guidance for current information about records you need to keep. Tax treatment depends on your individual circumstances.

This guide is general record-keeping information and does not determine whether a particular expense is deductible or how much tax you owe.

Use a repeatable session format

For each session, use the same fields: date, platform or source, gross income, hours online, kilometres and costs. Consistent categories make monthly comparisons easier and reduce the chance that a change in your own recording method looks like a change in profitability.

Separate profitability from tax records

A profitability record answers “was this work worthwhile after the costs I chose to allocate?” Tax records answer a different question about assessable income, deductible expenses and evidence under tax rules. Keeping those purposes distinct reduces the risk of treating every business cost estimate as a tax deduction.

Review trends, not just one session

One unusually busy or quiet session can distort your impression of gig work. Compare several weeks of income, hours and kilometres. The Delivery Profit Calculator is useful for individual scenarios, while your underlying records are what allow longer-term comparisons.

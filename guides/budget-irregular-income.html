How to Budget When Your Income Changes Each Week

A step-by-step budgeting method for Australian casual workers, shift workers and gig workers with variable income.

Separate predictable costs from flexible spending

Start with rent or mortgage payments, utilities, groceries, transport, insurance and minimum debt repayments. Convert monthly costs to a weekly planning amount by multiplying by 12 and dividing by 52. For example, a $130 monthly bill needs approximately $30 a week set aside.

Build a conservative income baseline

Look at several months of actual deposits rather than your best week. If take-home earnings in four sample weeks are $650, $900, $720 and $1,050, the average is $830. But using $650 as the amount available for essential commitments may provide more protection in a quiet week. Four weeks alone are not enough to predict seasonal work reliably.

Use separate buckets

A simple approach is to separate essentials, upcoming bills, a buffer and optional spending. For self-employed gig work, set aside a separate amount for tax and business expenses based on your circumstances; platform revenue is not the same as take-home profit.

Handle a strong week

When earnings exceed the conservative baseline, consider topping up upcoming bills and your buffer before increasing discretionary spending. If a week is weak, reduce optional spending before missing a necessary payment.

Plan around pay dates

List when bills are actually due. Even if monthly income exceeds monthly expenses, timing can create a shortfall. Keep a calendar of the next four to eight weeks, and update it after each pay day.

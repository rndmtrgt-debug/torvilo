WORKER GUIDE

How to check your pay

A useful pay check starts with your own shift records, the pay period on your payslip and the current award or agreement information that applies to your work.

1. Record what you actually worked

Keep the date of each shift, start and finish times, unpaid breaks and any other details that could affect the rate. Your own records make it easier to compare hours worked with the hours and payments shown by payroll.

You can use TORVILO's Shift Tracker to record paid hours, hourly rates and tips locally on your device. Exporting the tracker to CSV periodically gives you a separate copy of those entries.

2. Identify the award or agreement

For most employees, minimum pay is determined by an award covering their industry or occupation. A registered agreement may apply instead. Check your contract or payslip and ask your employer, HR or payroll team if you are unsure what instrument they are using.

Read Understanding Australian pay rates for more detail about awards, agreements, classifications and the National Minimum Wage.

3. Check your classification and employment type

Two people working for the same employer can have different minimum rates because their classifications, duties or employment types differ. Check whether you are full-time, part-time or casual and identify the classification being applied to your role.

4. Check the current official rate

Use the Fair Work Ombudsman minimum wages information and official pay guides. Make sure you are checking the rate that applied during the pay period in question, because rates can change.

5. Compare the same pay period

Match your shift records to the dates covered by the payslip. Separate ordinary hours from any hours that may attract overtime or penalty rates, and note allowances or other separately identified payments.

For a simple arithmetic check, the Shift Pay Calculator can estimate gross shift pay from a rate, shift length, unpaid break and multiplier you provide. The Paid Hours & Break Calculator can help calculate paid hours.

6. Read the payslip carefully

Fair Work says employees must receive a payslip within one working day of pay day. Payslips include information about the pay period and gross and net pay, along with other required payment details.

If something does not match

Recheck the dates, hours, rate, classification and any penalty or overtime rules first. If there is still a difference, keep your records and payslips together and raise the specific discrepancy with your employer or payroll team. For questions about minimum entitlements, use current Fair Work information or seek appropriate professional advice.

TORVILO calculations are arithmetic estimates, not a determination of your legal entitlement. Awards, agreements and rates can change.

7. Reconcile gross pay before net pay

If the hours and rates appear correct, calculate the expected gross amount before investigating the bank deposit. Gross pay tests the wage arithmetic; net pay introduces deductions as another variable. Separating those stages can quickly narrow down where a difference begins.

8. Keep a clear record of the question

If you raise a discrepancy, note the pay period, shift date, hours, rate you expected and the payslip amount you are comparing. Keep any response with the same records. This creates a simple history if the issue needs to be revisited.

A repeatable payday routine

Each payday, match the payslip period to your shift records, check hours, check rate categories, compare gross pay, review deductions and then look at net pay and super. The process usually takes less time when records are maintained as you work rather than reconstructed later.

For the terminology behind the figures, read Gross pay vs net pay. For a stronger personal record, see Keeping useful work and pay records.
